# Triage and nursing

For triage nurses, nurses, nursing officers in charge and midwives. Triage decides who is seen first, so accurate vital signs and categories save lives.

## The triage queue

- Open **Clinical → Triage**. Patients checked in at reception appear in arrival order as *Waiting triage*.
- Select a patient to open their triage form. Their banner shows age, sex, allergies and payer at the top.

## Recording vital signs

Start with the patient's **chief complaint** in their own words. Then record every vital sign you measure, in the units shown:

- Temperature (°C), pulse (beats/min), respiratory rate (breaths/min)
- Blood pressure (systolic/diastolic, mmHg) and blood glucose (mmol/L) where indicated
- Weight (kg) and height (cm). For children they feed growth monitoring.
- Pain score (0–10) and any notes on how the patient presents

**Re-check any value that looks unusual** before saving; a mistyped value can mislead the clinician. Leave a field empty rather than guessing.

## Choosing the triage category

Choose the category that matches the patient's condition, not the order they arrived in:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Red, Immediate** | Life-threatening; see now (e.g. not breathing, shock, active seizure) |
| **Orange, Very urgent** | Could deteriorate quickly; see within minutes |
| **Yellow, Urgent** | Needs prompt assessment |
| **Green, Standard** | Stable; routine wait |
| **Blue, Non-urgent** | Minor problem |

Press **Save**. The visit moves to *Waiting for doctor*. **Red and orange** make the visit an **Emergency** and **yellow** makes it **Urgent**. Emergency and urgent patients are shown first, highlighted, in the triage queue and on the doctor's worklist; everyone else follows in arrival order.

> Emergencies should not wait in the triage queue. Bring them straight to **Emergency** and record the details as soon as the patient is safe.

## Nursing on the ward

- The **Ward board** shows every bed: occupied, free, reserved or being cleaned. It also shows each patient's nursing tasks.
- Record observations and medication given **at the time you do them**, not at the end of the shift. The record is legal evidence of care.
- Bed status changes when patients are admitted, transferred or discharged. Mark a bed clean once it is ready, so admissions can use it.
- Hand over using the ward board, not paper lists, so the next shift sees the same information.

## Maternal and child health (midwives and MCH nurses)

- **ANC/PNC clinic** holds each mother's antenatal and postnatal visits (these feed MOH 405 and 406).
- **Labour ward** holds the partograph. Record cervical dilatation, fetal heart rate, contractions and maternal observations at the intervals shown. The chart warns when progress crosses the alert and action lines.
- Record the delivery outcome promptly. A live birth **automatically enrols the baby in the Child welfare clinic** with the KEPI immunisation schedule, and produces the birth notification.
