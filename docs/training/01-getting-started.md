# Getting started with HMIS

Everyone who uses HMIS completes this module first. It covers how you get your account, how to sign in safely, and how to find your way around.

## Your account and first sign-in

HMIS accounts are created by your hospital's administrator. You never create your own account for work.

1. Your administrator adds you under **Admin → Staff** with your name, work email, role, facility and department.
2. You receive an email titled **"You've been added to [your hospital]"**.
3. Click **Set your password** in the email. The link works **once** and expires after **72 hours**.
4. Choose a strong password. You are signed in straight away and taken to the page for your role.

If the link has expired, ask your administrator to press **Resend invite**, or use **Forgot your password?** on the login page.

> **Tip:** Your login email is your work email. Never share your password, even with a colleague or supervisor. Every action in HMIS is recorded against the person who is signed in.

## Signing in and staying secure

- Go to the system address your hospital gave you (for example `https://hmis.solforbs.com`) and sign in with your email and password.
- Turn on **two-factor authentication** or add a **passkey** under **Settings → Security**. This stops anyone who learns your password from signing in.
- Your hospital may set an **idle timeout**. If you leave the screen unattended for that long, you are signed out automatically and must sign in again.
- Your hospital may also allow sign-in only from **approved networks** (for example, the hospital Wi-Fi). If you see "Access to … is restricted to approved networks", you are outside them.
- Always **log out** (your name at the bottom of the menu → Log out) on shared computers.

## Finding your way around

- **The menu on the left** shows only the areas your role is allowed to use, grouped as Front office, Clinical, Diagnostics, Pharmacy, Maternity, Inpatient, Finance, Management and Administration.
- What you see also depends on your facility's **KEPH level** (1–6). For example, theatre and radiology only appear at facilities offering them.
- **Your dashboard** shows today's work for your role, such as queues, pending results and alerts.
- **Notifications** (the bell) carry clinical alerts, such as a critical result or a patient sent to your department.
- **Lists update by themselves.** Queues and worklists refresh within a few seconds when a colleague moves a patient, so you rarely need to reload the page.
- If you work at more than one hospital, switch between them on your **dashboard**.

## How a patient moves through HMIS

Every outpatient visit (called an **encounter**) moves through clear stages. Each department sees patients waiting at its stage:

1. **Reception** registers or finds the patient and checks them in. The visit is now *Waiting triage*.
2. **Triage** records vital signs and a triage category. The visit is now *Waiting for doctor*.
3. **Consultation**: the clinician documents, diagnoses (ICD-11), orders tests and prescribes. They can send the patient to *Awaiting results*, *Waiting pharmacy* or *Waiting billing*, request admission, or refer.
4. **Laboratory / Radiology** process the orders; released results return to the clinician.
5. **Pharmacy** dispenses the prescription.
6. **Cashier** takes payment, or the visit is claimed from SHA or an insurer.
7. The clinician **completes** the visit. The record is signed, and for SHA patients it is shared with the national Shared Health Record.

Admitted patients continue on the ward and are discharged with a discharge summary.

## Getting help

- Each page has short instructions next to the fields that need them.
- Error pages explain what happened and give you a way back, for example "Access not allowed" tells you to ask your administrator to update your role.
- For access problems, speak to your **hospital administrator**. For system faults, your administrator contacts the HMIS support team.
- Complete the training modules for your role under **Help & training**. Your administrator can see your training progress.
