# Privacy, confidentiality and security

Health information is **sensitive personal data** under Kenya's **Data Protection Act, 2019**, and the **Digital Health Act, 2023** requires it to be handled securely. This module is required for every user.

## Your legal duty of confidentiality

- Access a patient's record **only when you are involved in their care** or have a work reason to (for example, billing that visit).
- Do not look up family, friends, colleagues, celebrities or yourself out of curiosity. This is a breach even if you tell no one.
- Share information only with people who need it for care, and only through HMIS or approved channels. Do not send patient details over personal WhatsApp, SMS or email.
- Do not photograph screens or printouts, and collect printouts from the printer immediately.
- Patients have rights under the Act: to be informed, to access their records, and to have errors corrected. Direct such requests to your records officer or administrator.

## Everything you do is recorded

HMIS keeps an **audit log** of sign-ins, record views and changes, with who did it, when, and from which network address.

- Administrators and auditors review the audit log under **Management → Audit log**.
- Clinical notes are **signed**. After signing, a note cannot be silently changed: an amendment creates a new version and the original stays on record.
- Because the log identifies you, never let anyone work under your account, and never use theirs.

## How HMIS protects data

- **Encrypted identifiers.** National ID, Client Registry (CR) and SHA numbers are encrypted in the database.
- **Each hospital's data is separate.** Staff of one hospital cannot see another hospital's patients.
- **Least privilege.** Your role gives you only the permissions your job needs. If you need more, your administrator must change your role. Do not borrow an account.
- **Secure connections only (HTTPS).** Sessions time out, and sign-in can be limited to approved networks.
- **Search engines are blocked** from every page that shows patient information.

## Support access ("break-glass")

Sometimes the HMIS support team needs to look at your hospital's data to fix a reported problem. This is called **break-glass access**, and it is tightly controlled:

- It must have a written **reason** and is **time-limited** (for example, 30 minutes).
- It is **read-only**: support staff cannot change or add records.
- It is **recorded in the audit log**, and your hospital's administrators are **notified** when it starts.

If you see a notification about support access that nobody requested, tell your administrator immediately.

## Reporting incidents

Report these to your administrator or data protection lead **immediately**:

- You think someone knows your password, or you see sign-ins you did not make.
- You sent patient information to the wrong person.
- A device holding patient information (phone, laptop, printout) was lost or stolen.
- You notice a record that looks wrong or was changed unexpectedly.

The Data Protection Act requires certain breaches to be reported to the **Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)** within **72 hours**. Fast internal reporting makes that possible.
